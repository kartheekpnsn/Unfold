# Research: Ambani vs Adani

- Slug: Ambani-Vs-Adani · Duration: 30m · Researched: 2026-11-23 · Sources: 12
- Focus (from probe): How Mukesh Ambani's Reliance and Gautam Adani's portfolio make money, organise businesses, finance expansion and face different risks—not wealth rankings, personality judgements or investment advice.

## 1. Key concepts (dependency chain)
1. **People, companies and reporting boundaries**: A leader is not a financial statement; distinguish Reliance Industries Limited (RIL), its consolidated businesses, Adani Enterprises Limited (AEL), and the wider Adani portfolio. Consolidation represents a parent and controlled subsidiaries as one economic entity. · [S1] [S2] [S3]
2. **Money vocabulary** (needs 1): Cash flow must be distinguished from accounting profit; operating, investing and financing cash flows answer different questions. · [S4]
3. **Reliance's earning engines** (needs 1–2): Fuels and chemicals, digital connectivity, retail and other segments earn money through different products, customers and margins. · [S1] [S5] [S6] [S7]
4. **Adani's earning engines** (needs 1–2): Ports/logistics, airports, renewable power and other infrastructure sit in distinct companies or subsidiaries, rather than all inside AEL. · [S3] [S8] [S9] [S10]
5. **Growth and integration** (needs 3–4): Building or buying connected assets can expand capacity and customer access, but investment precedes the full earnings contribution. · [S5] [S8] [S10]
6. **Financing and cash discipline** (needs 2, 5): Expansion uses operating resources, equity and borrowing; net debt, maturities and cash availability matter at the relevant entity boundary. · [S4] [S5] [S8] [S10]
7. **Risk and standards of evidence** (needs 1–6): Test each earning engine and distinguish management expectations, reported results, allegations and the precise scope of court decisions. · [S3] [S6] [S7] [S11] [S12]

## 2. Claims
### People, companies and reporting boundaries
- Mukesh D. Ambani is identified as RIL's Chairman and Managing Director in its FY2025–26 report; the report describes a business portfolio spanning energy/materials, digital services, retail, media and emerging technologies. · [S1]
- Gautam Adani is identified as Chairman of the Adani Group in AEL's 30 April 2026 results release; AEL calls itself the group's flagship company, not the consolidated parent of every Adani listed business. · [S3]
- The general consolidation principle is control: consolidated statements represent a parent and its subsidiaries' assets, liabilities, income, expenses and cash flows as one economic entity; sharing a leader or brand is not that definition. · [S2]
- AEL describes its strategy as developing infrastructure businesses and separating them into listed entities, naming Ports, Energy Solutions, Power, Green Energy and Total Gas among its earlier businesses. · [S3]
- The Adani portfolio release groups utility companies, APSEZ, AEL and cement businesses; its portfolio totals must therefore remain separately labelled, rather than being presented as AEL consolidated figures or an automatically equivalent RIL-style consolidation. · [S3] [S10] [S2]

### Money vocabulary
- Cash-flow statements classify movements into operating activities, acquisition/disposal of long-term assets and investments, and changes in equity/borrowings—respectively operating, investing and financing. · [S4]
- The indirect cash-flow method adjusts accounting profit for non-cash transactions, accruals/deferrals and investing or financing items; a profit figure alone therefore does not establish cash generated from operations. · [S4]
- Adani's portfolio release defines its EBITDA by adding specified items to PAT, including share of JV profit, taxes, depreciation, finance cost, forex adjustments and exceptional items; this is a disclosed company measure, not cash in a bank account. · [S10] [S4]
- RIL reports FY2025–26 consolidated revenue of ₹11,75,919 crore, EBITDA of ₹2,07,911 crore and PAT of ₹95,754 crore; its standalone revenue is ₹5,46,852 crore, illustrating how changing the boundary changes the answer. · [S5]
- For comparison, use the full financial year ending 31 March 2026 and rupees in crore; RIL's revenue, AEL's total income and AGEL's power-supply revenue retain their original labels rather than being silently treated as identical measures. · [S5] [S3] [S8] [S9]

### Reliance's earning engines
- RIL's Oil-to-Chemicals (O2C) segment reports FY2025–26 revenue of ₹6,62,401 crore and EBITDA of ₹60,546 crore; fuel-margin conditions and feedstock sourcing are identified as earnings drivers. · [S1] [S5]
- O2C economics depend on product/feedstock margins, not merely the direction of crude prices: RIL reports supportive transportation-fuel margins while downstream chemical margins remained subdued. · [S5]
- Digital Services reports revenue from operations of ₹1,49,965 crore and EBITDA of ₹76,560 crore in FY2025–26; subscriber additions, average revenue per user (ARPU) growth and digital-service expansion supported growth. · [S6]
- Reliance Retail sells across electronics, fashion and grocery through stores and digital platforms; FY2025–26 revenue from operations was ₹3,28,202 crore and EBITDA ₹27,034 crore. · [S7]
- Digital Services' reported EBITDA margin was 51.1%, while Retail's was 8.2%, both calculated on revenue from operations; their respective value-of-sales-and-services figures were ₹1,76,164 crore and ₹3,71,085 crore, not those margin denominators. · [S6] [S7]
- Oil and Gas is a separate earning engine from O2C: RIL attributes its FY2025–26 EBITDA decline of 10.1% to natural production decline in KG D6 and softer gas prices. · [S5]

### Adani's earning engines
- APSEZ's consolidated FY2025–26 results report revenue of ₹38,736 crore and EBITDA of ₹22,851 crore; port cargo handled was 500.8 million metric tonnes (MMT), an operating-volume measure rather than money. · [S8]
- APSEZ's shore-to-door model links port handling, rail, logistics parks, warehousing and road delivery; FY2025–26 logistics revenue was ₹4,478 crore and marine revenue ₹2,681 crore. · [S8]
- Airports are an AEL incubating business: their FY2025–26 total income was ₹13,081 crore and EBITDA ₹5,394 crore, while passenger movements were 95.3 million. · [S3]
- AGEL develops, owns and operates grid-connected solar, wind, hybrid and storage assets; FY2025–26 power-supply revenue was ₹11,602 crore and power-supply EBITDA ₹10,865 crore, with energy sales of 37,567 million units. · [S9]
- AGEL's power-supply EBITDA includes carbon-credit income and prompt-payment discount and excludes specified expenses; its reported 91% margin should not be reconstructed by simply dividing the two headline figures or treated as a net-profit margin. · [S9]
- The broader Adani portfolio also includes thermal power, energy transmission/distribution and smart meters, gas and cement businesses; ports and renewables are teaching examples, not a complete group earnings map. · [S10]

### Growth and integration
- Reliance links connectivity and commerce through retail distribution of Jio services and devices; Retail also uses stores for JioMart hyper-local fulfilment. This is a concrete integration mechanism, not evidence that every subscriber becomes a retail customer. · [S7]
- RIL reports FY2025–26 capex of ₹1,44,271 crore, mainly for O2C/New Energy growth projects and continuing Digital Services/Retail expansion. · [S5]
- APSEZ grew through both operating expansion and acquisitions: its release attributes international growth partly to NQXT Australia and the Colombo ramp-up, while noting that NQXT entered consolidation only in Q4. · [S8]
- Adani reports portfolio FY2025–26 capex of ₹1,52,967 crore and EBITDA of ₹94,834 crore; its 2 June 2026 release says newly commissioned assets' contributions would become fuller later, and reports AEL raising ₹24,930 crore through a rights issue. · [S10]

### Financing and cash discipline
- At 31 March 2026, RIL reported gross debt of ₹3,74,421 crore and net debt of ₹1,24,717 crore; a discussion of its balance sheet must distinguish the two. · [S5]
- RIL says capex was adequately funded by cash profits of ₹1,71,258 crore, but this management statement does not turn “cash profits minus capex” into verified free cash flow; operating cash-flow reconciliation is still required. · [S5] [S4]
- RIL also raised financing to refinance maturities and fund capital expenditure, including export-credit-agency-supported facilities; internally funded growth does not mean absence of borrowing. · [S5]
- APSEZ reports gross debt of ₹55,103 crore, cash balance of ₹12,193 crore, net debt/EBITDA of 1.9x and average debt maturity of 5.4 years at 31 March 2026; these describe APSEZ, not every Adani entity. · [S8]
- Adani's portfolio reports net debt/EBITDA of 3.3x and cash of ₹55,852 crore at FY2025–26 end; AGEL separately defines “cash profit” as PAT plus depreciation, deferred tax and exceptional items, not an operating cash-flow statement. · [S10] [S9] [S4]

### Risk and standards of evidence
- Jio identifies technology obsolescence, supply-chain disruption and irrational price competition as risks; Retail identifies weaker consumer sentiment, discretionary spending and sourcing/logistics disruptions. · [S6] [S7]
- APSEZ's release discusses tariff uncertainty and geopolitical disruption and distinguishes acquisition-affected return metrics; teaching inference: neither cargo growth nor a high operating margin alone guarantees a good return on the capital invested. · [S8]
- AEL's FY2025–26 EBITDA declined 2% to ₹16,464 crore despite total income increasing 3% to ₹1,02,943 crore; its Q4 attributable PAT was negative ₹221 crore, with the release identifying depreciation on recently commissioned airport and copper assets. · [S3]
- Renewable operating capacity is not the same as cash or electricity sold: AGEL reports both 19.3 GW of capacity and 37,567 million units of annual energy sales, and measures PPA-based generation against contractual commitments. · [S9]
- The court order dated 10 August 2026 dismissed Counts Two, Three and Four with prejudice against Gautam Adani, Sagar Adani and Vneet Jaain; it reserved judgment on Counts One and Five against five other defendants. AGEL's accompanying filing says the company itself was not a party to that criminal proceeding. · [S11]
- Reuters reports that Adani Group denied wrongdoing, and the attached court order explicitly says dismissal expresses no opinion on the merits; neither the earlier allegations nor this procedural outcome should be taught as a proven verdict about every Adani business. · [S12] [S11]

## 3. Analogies for ELI5
These are original teaching devices, not quotations or factual descriptions of corporate ownership.

1. **Labelled lunchboxes—preferred opening**: Put RIL's consolidated businesses inside one reporting outline; place distinct Adani company lunchboxes beneath a dotted portfolio banner. Changing the outline changes the totals. Pick this because the reporting boundary is the biggest trap. · where it breaks: real subsidiaries, joint ventures and shared ownership are more complex; lunchbox size does not represent value or leader wealth. · [S2] [S3] [S10]
2. **Three cash pipes**: A customer pipe brings operating receipts, a construction pipe spends on assets, and a financing pipe carries loans/equity in and repayments out. Put EBITDA on a dashboard outside the tank to show it is not the tank balance. · where it breaks: the direct cash receipts/payments picture omits accruals and non-cash accounting; use the reconciliation next. · [S4]
3. **Connected railway stations and a neighbourhood marketplace**: Use the Adani ports chain as connected service stations and Reliance's stores/Jio network as connected customer touchpoints. Pick this for integration after boundaries are clear. · where it breaks: Reliance also has industrial infrastructure and Adani also has consumer-facing airports; neither is exclusively one model. · [S1] [S3] [S7] [S8]

## 5. Common misconceptions
- **Myth:** “Ambani's or Adani's personal wealth is company cash.” · **Why it's wrong:** Corporate reports describe entities' business results and cash/debt, while leader roles are separately identified. · **Correct idea:** Ask which company and which statement before discussing money; this pack does not measure personal wealth. · [S1] [S3] [S4] [S5]
- **Myth:** “Adani Enterprises is the whole Adani Group.” · **Why it's wrong:** AEL describes earlier businesses separated into listed companies. · **Correct idea:** Draw AEL, APSEZ, AGEL and other portfolio companies as distinct reporting boxes. · [S3] [S10]
- **Myth:** “Revenue, EBITDA, PAT and cash profit are interchangeable.” · **Why it's wrong:** RIL reports different revenue/EBITDA/PAT values, AGEL defines cash profit with non-cash add-backs, and actual cash flows need reconciliation. · **Correct idea:** Name the measure and what it leaves out. · [S5] [S9] [S4]
- **Myth:** “AGEL's 91% EBITDA margin means 91% is distributable profit.” · **Why it's wrong:** It is an adjusted power-supply operating measure, not PAT or cash after construction and financing. · **Correct idea:** Read the definition, then the cash-flow and financing statements. · [S9] [S4]
- **Myth:** “All growth comes from debt” or “internally funded growth means no debt.” · **Why it's wrong:** Adani reports an AEL rights issue, and Reliance reports both cash-profit-funded capex and refinancing/capex facilities. · **Correct idea:** Separate operating resources, equity and borrowing. · [S10] [S5]
- **Myth:** “The Adani indictment proves every company committed fraud” or “dismissal proves every allegation false.” · **Why it's wrong:** The criminal matter concerned individuals, the order has count-specific scope, and its footnote expressly disclaims a merits opinion. · **Correct idea:** Describe allegations, denials and dated dispositions separately. · [S11] [S12]
- **Myth:** “The bigger top line is the universal winner.” · **Why it's wrong:** AEL total income and AGEL power-supply revenue are differently scoped, and Digital/Retail margins use operating-revenue denominators. · **Correct idea:** Compare a chosen business question with matching boundaries and definitions, not a personality contest. · [S3] [S6] [S7] [S9]

## 6. Trade-offs / when (not) to use
**Debates and perspectives**—the analytical lenses below are teaching choices, not claims that named market participants endorse a winner.

- **Diversification/integration perspective—Reliance management:** RIL attributes performance to a diversified portfolio and describes rising digital/consumer activity alongside industrial earnings. **Benefit:** different earning engines and linked distribution. **Counterweight:** Jio's competition/technology risks and Retail's spending/supply-chain risks do not disappear through diversification. · [S5] [S6] [S7]
- **Infrastructure-growth perspective—Adani management:** The portfolio release emphasises commissioning assets, core-infrastructure earnings and future contribution from new investments. **Benefit:** expanding service capacity and connected transport/utility platforms. **Counterweight:** capex precedes full contribution; AEL's results show depreciation affecting profit after new assets begin operating. Future contribution remains a management expectation. · [S10] [S3]
- **Cash-accountability perspective—IFRS Foundation's statement framework:** Use operating/investing/financing reconciliation to test whether reported earnings support commitments. **Benefit:** avoids mistaking cash profit or EBITDA for spendable cash. **Counterweight:** the framework alone does not establish a company's cash generation; entity statements and notes still need inspection. · [S4] [S9]
- **Portfolio-summary versus entity-credit perspective—Adani portfolio release and APSEZ management:** Portfolio leverage provides broad context, while APSEZ separately reports 1.9x net debt/EBITDA and maturity information. **Benefit:** two scales of analysis. **Counterweight:** aggregate cash is not established here as freely transferable to every borrower, nor is 1.9x every Adani company's ratio. · [S10] [S8]
- **When to use:** Use same-year entity cards to explain business mechanisms and financing; use the separately labelled portfolio card only for broad context. **When not to use:** Do not infer share valuation, personal wealth, a universal winner or identical credit risk from these figures. **Alternative:** compare one sector/entity's operating volumes, operating cash flow and capital employed when the question is narrower. · [S2] [S3] [S4] [S8] [S9]
- **Legal interpretation—company communication versus judicial text:** AGEL's cover letter emphasises dismissal; the attached court order specifies the dismissed counts, reserves others and disclaims a merits judgment. Prefer the court's exact scope for the lesson, corroborated by Reuters, rather than generalising a headline to all defendants or civil proceedings. · [S11] [S12]

## 9. Suggested quiz items
- Reporting boundary: Does AEL's consolidated EBITDA include every Adani listed company? · answer: No; AEL and the broader portfolio are distinct, and AEL describes separately listed former incubations. · common wrong answer: Yes, because AEL is the flagship. · [S3] [S10]
- Consolidation: What determines the basic parent/subsidiary consolidation boundary? · answer: Control, not merely a shared business-group brand or leader. · common wrong answer: Any business carrying the same surname or brand. · [S2]
- Money vocabulary: Is a loan received customer revenue? · answer: It is financing cash flow, not operating customer receipts. · common wrong answer: All cash entering the business is sales. · [S4]
- Profit versus cash: Can AGEL cash profit be called operating cash flow without checking a reconciliation? · answer: No; its definition adds back accounting items and does not replace the cash-flow statement. · common wrong answer: Yes, because “cash” is in the name. · [S9] [S4]
- Reliance economics: If crude rises, must O2C profit rise? · answer: No; fuel/product margins, feedstock costs and chemical conditions matter. · common wrong answer: An oil-related business always benefits from higher oil prices. · [S5]
- Margin denominator: Which FY2025–26 Digital Services figure underlies the reported 51.1% EBITDA margin? · answer: Revenue from operations of ₹1,49,965 crore. · common wrong answer: Value of sales and services of ₹1,76,164 crore. · [S6]
- Adani engines: Where do the airport results used here sit? · answer: In AEL's incubating businesses, not APSEZ's ports results. · common wrong answer: All transport businesses are APSEZ. · [S3] [S8]
- Capacity versus sales: Is 19.3 GW AGEL's annual revenue? · answer: No, it is operating capacity; money and electricity sold have separate units. · common wrong answer: GW directly measures cash earned. · [S9]
- Debt boundary: Can APSEZ's 1.9x net debt/EBITDA be assigned to all Adani companies? · answer: No; it is entity-specific, while the portfolio separately reports 3.3x. · common wrong answer: A business group has one leverage ratio for every company. · [S8] [S10]
- Investment timing: Why can newly commissioned assets increase activity while reducing reported profit? · answer: Depreciation affects profit; AEL identifies this effect for its airport/copper assets in Q4 FY2025–26. · common wrong answer: Any completed asset must immediately raise PAT. · [S3]
- Evidence: Does the August 2026 dismissal establish a merits verdict for every allegation and defendant? · answer: No; the order dismisses specified counts against three people, reserves others and disclaims a merits opinion. · common wrong answer: Every allegation was proved false, or every Adani company was convicted. · [S11] [S12]
- Fair comparison: Why not rank RIL against AGEL using their headline revenue and EBITDA margins? · answer: The boundaries and measures differ—RIL consolidation versus AGEL's adjusted power-supply activity. · common wrong answer: Same currency makes every number comparable. · [S5] [S9]

## 10. Gaps and conflicts
- **Annual baseline, not latest trading performance:** FY2025–26 is the common full-year baseline. This pack does not establish FY2026–27 quarterly developments, current market capitalisations, personal fortunes or investment returns; these are unnecessary for the probe's business-model question. · [S3] [S5] [S8] [S9]
- **Cash-flow gap:** Audited operating cash-flow totals and cash-capex reconciliations were not extracted for all entities. Do not calculate or rank free cash flow using EBITDA or “cash profit minus capex.” RIL's funding statement and AGEL's cash-profit definition are reported faithfully but remain different from operating cash-flow evidence. · [S5] [S9] [S4]
- **Portfolio comparability:** Adani's ₹94,834 crore EBITDA and ₹1,52,967 crore capex are company-defined portfolio disclosures. The extracted HTML does not establish a RIL-equivalent audited consolidation perimeter, all eliminations or the same EBITDA adjustments. Do not sum listed-company figures indiscriminately or declare the portfolio's capex a universally comparable record. · [S10] [S2]
- **Prior-year presentation:** AEL's FY2025–26 release presents FY2024–25 EBITDA as ₹16,722 crore. The pack uses that release's same-basis comparator, not a number stitched from an earlier release with a potentially different business perimeter. Exact adjustments were not independently reconstructed. · [S3]
- **APSEZ guidance wording:** The release says it “surpassed” guidance, but its own table shows cargo of 500.8 MMT against 505–515 MMT guidance and capex of ₹15,320 crore against ₹11,000–12,000 crore. Teach the reported numbers: exceeding a spending estimate is not automatically a benefit, and not every target was beaten. · [S8]
- **Legal scope conflict:** AGEL's cover communication frames a broad dismissal, but the attached order dismisses Counts Two–Four against the three appearing defendants and reserves Counts One/Five against others. The judicial text is more precise and controls this account; Reuters independently corroborates the central dismissal and merits caveat. · [S11] [S12]
- **Legal freshness and separate proceedings:** No later court order resolving the reserved counts was verified; the August order's 31 August deadline does not establish what happened afterward. Reuters reports separate SEC and sanctions resolutions, but primary settlement documents were not successfully read; omit their detailed amounts from the lesson and do not present criminal dismissal as disposing of every civil/regulatory matter. · [S11] [S12]
- **Access failures:** SEC pages returned 403; the DOJ release returned an interstitial rather than substantive text; a Business Standard report was blocked. None is counted as a verified source. The AGEL court attachment was fetched and its cover/operative pages extracted from the PDF in memory; sources below are the pages/documents whose relevant substantive content was actually read.
- **Coverage boundary:** Exact promoter holdings, intra-group guarantees, restrictions on cash transfers, detailed tariff/concession terms, AGEL's full merchant/PPA split, environmental approvals and rating-agency methodologies were not verified. Do not invent them or infer identical financing/security arrangements across entities. · [S2] [S3] [S8] [S9] [S10]
- **Trust and bias:** Eleven sources are primary, including the IFRS Foundation's conceptual standards summaries and a company-hosted court-order attachment; one is Reuters secondary reporting. Corporate releases establish what issuers report, not independent validation of marketing superlatives, future project success or governance quality. IFRS summaries explain concepts, not a claim that Indian issuers directly report under IFRS rather than their applicable local standards. · [S2] [S4] [S8] [S9] [S10] [S11] [S12]
